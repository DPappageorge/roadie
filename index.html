Track 01The Thesis

Find the audience. Show them the artist. Give clear ways to support them.

That's the whole job. It hasn't changed since the music industry began — only the tools to do it have.

The soul of the music industry is taking a great artist, finding their audience, showing that audience the artist, and giving them clearly defined ways to support the artist. That's it. — the founder's stated thesis for this project
A buddy for life — owned and operated by you.

Roadie performs that same timeless job — done by an AI agent, running continuously, using the actual tools of the modern age: Meta's algorithmic targeting, Spotify's stream count, automated creative testing. Not a manager's phone calls or a label's radio budget.

It isn't a campaign bought once. It runs indefinitely, following the artist through their entire career, putting them in control of professional-grade marketing even if they know nothing about marketing.

If the artist gets signed, they can grant their label or manager temporary, restricted access without ever losing ownership — the account stays theirs; access is a permission they grant and can revoke, not a transfer.

Track 02The Gap

DIY musicians have the platforms. Nobody's built the glue.

Meta can find an audience for almost anything. Spotify hosts the fans. Neither one talks to the other — and running paid acquisition well takes a skill set most independent artists have no time to learn: account setup, audience segmentation, creative testing, budget pacing, day-to-day optimization.

The tools already exist. What's missing is an operator that runs them continuously, on the artist's behalf, without requiring the artist to become an ad buyer.

Track 04The Mechanism

Two audiences, one hard rule, one soft hint.

Pool A Existing fans
hard-excluded from, softly seeds
Pool B Potential fans
Built from

Pool A — page & Instagram engagers, email list, site visitors. Pool B — a Meta Lookalike seeded from Pool A, offered to the algorithm only as a hint.

What's actually real in 2026

Under Meta's Andromeda update, only location, minimum age, language, custom-audience exclusion, and Special Ad Category are hard controls — the Lookalike itself is a suggestion the algorithm can override. Decision: trust Meta's own targeting, and put the real effort into exclusion, the conversion objective, creative variety, and budget concentration instead of manual audience-picking.

Track 05The Test Case

Two goals, proven on one artist first.

Before this is a product for anyone, it has to work for someone.

Goal 1

Two fan pools

Build and grow Pool A (existing fans) and Pool B (potential fans) — the audience infrastructure everything else runs on.

Goal 2

Real streaming numbers

Not for ad revenue — for leverage. Streams become currency in rooms that don't care about impressions: label conversations, licensing, booking.

Scope is deliberately narrow: Meta only, Spotify only, one artist's own ad account, starting from a modest existing following rather than an established fanbase — a true cold-start case, since followers aren't a Custom Audience until they've actually engaged. That narrowing removes the money-custody question entirely for phase one and forces the cold-start plan to be real, not theoretical.

Track 07Decisions Log

What was in the way — and what we decided.

StructuralResolved

Attribution gap

Meta can't see past the click into Spotify, and Spotify locked down its developer API in February 2026.

Decision

A smart-link bridge page fires a Meta Pixel "confirmed click-through" event as the real optimization target — stronger than a raw ad click. Native video/ThruPlay engagement serves as a secondary, no-pixel-needed signal. Real stream numbers come from Spotify for Artists' own CSV export, uploaded periodically and overlaid against spend — shown as directional correlation, not a precise ROAS figure it can't back. Good enough, because the actual goal is leverage in real conversations, not ad ROI.

StructuralResolved

Targeting shifted underneath the idea

Meta cut detailed-interest categories in Jan–Feb 2026, and under the Andromeda update even Lookalike seeding is now a soft suggestion, not a hard constraint.

Decision

Trust Meta's own algorithm for the "who." Concentrate the product's effort on what's still real: hard-excluding Pool A from cold campaigns, choosing the right conversion objective, creative testing velocity, and budget concentration (fragmenting into narrow ad sets is a confirmed anti-pattern). The long-term edge is cross-artist creative-performance learning at scale, not out-targeting Meta per account.

GateResolved

Rights Manager risk

Paid ads featuring the song's actual audio can be auto-flagged even when the artist owns the track.

Decision

Static/image creative for the proof of concept — zero exposure. Video comes next, fed to Meta's Dynamic Creative / Advantage+ Creative as a varied batch, not one hero cut. Audio-inclusive video is gated on Meta Rights Manager "allowlisting," typically obtained through the artist's own distributor — action item to confirm what the founder's distributor offers.

GateResolved

Cold start

A brand-new artist has no existing engagement to seed Pool A from.

Decision

Validated against a realistic starting point — a modest existing following, well short of what's needed for a usable audience on day one. Plan: one broad engagement campaign rather than narrowly targeting a small follower base, a parallel push to convert followers into an owned email list, and holding the stream-drive campaign until Pool A is real. Early weeks are judged on audience growth and cost-per-result, not stream lift.

GateResolved

Meta App Review

Managing other artists' ad accounts requires Meta's Advanced Access review, not just a personal token.

Decision

Two parallel tracks: Business Verification (needs a registered business entity, days to weeks) and App Review for ads_management Advanced Access (use-case writeup, screencast demo, 3–7 business days per cycle, revisions likely). The Marketing API Access Tier starts at Limited — Full Access requires 500+ prior API calls, a gate only real usage can clear. Both tracks start the moment there's a working demo, run in parallel with building, not after.

OpenResolved

Money custody + delegated access

Pooled agency accounts are the smoothest onboarding UX but conflict with "the artist owns this for life."

Decision

Committed to artist-owned Meta Business Manager / ad accounts — never pooled under Roadie's own account. Funding is streamlined with a Stripe Issuing virtual card scoped to the artist's balance, but attached to their account, not Roadie's — hard spend caps without Roadie ever becoming the advertiser of record. Labels and managers get scoped, revocable access via Meta's native Business Manager Partner Access — a platform primitive, not something to build. This is what makes "ownership follows the artist for their whole career" structurally true.

Track 08Sequence

Prove it small before it's a product.

Phase 0

Personal proof of concept

Meta + Spotify only, the artist's own ad account, static creative, a realistic cold-start position. Prove the pool mechanism, the smart-link conversion event, and the budget-allocation loop actually run.

Phase 1

Validate the signal

Does confirmed click-through volume correlate with real stream lift over several weeks, checked against Spotify for Artists' CSV export.

Phase 2

Clear the account-access gate

File Business Verification and Meta App Review for Advanced Access in parallel with building — budget multiple weeks including at least one revision cycle. Design the automation loop to be API-call-efficient while running on Limited Access tier.

Phase 3

Fund and onboard other artists

Guided setup wizard for the artist's own Meta Business Manager, Stripe Issuing card scoped to their balance, and a Partner Access flow for granting labels/managers scoped, revocable access.
